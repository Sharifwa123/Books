# Chapter 41 solutions

## Level 1
**1.1** *Watch*: subscribe to a repository's activity. *Star*: save the repository and show appreciation. *Follow*: see a person's public activity on your dashboard. Only watching subscribes you to notifications.

**1.2** Answers vary with the labels on your screen. The documentation names the inbox at `github.com/notifications` and the list of watched repositories at `github.com/watching`.

## Level 2
**2.1** For example: you were assigned to the issue or pull request, you opened it, you commented on it, you were mentioned (or a team you belong to was), or you changed its state.

**2.2** You will no longer receive notifications about activity that does not involve you: new issues and pull requests that you did not take part in, and similar general activity. You still receive conversations you are in and mentions.

## Level 3
**3.1** `reason:review-requested`.

**3.2** Answers vary. The point is to record the numbers.

## Level 4
**4.1** A good plan: (1) watch all activity only where you maintain or depend on the code; (2) elsewhere choose participating and mentions; (3) start each day from `reason:mention` and `reason:review-requested`; (4) mark handled notifications Done and Save those that must wait; (5) use an address you control, and review the watched list monthly.

## Level 5
**5.1** With **Ignore**, GitHub sends nothing, and the documentation says that includes mentions ("you won't be notified if you're @mentioned"). They should have chosen the level that notifies only when they take part or are mentioned.

**5.2** Notifications that are not marked Saved are kept for three months. Notifications they wanted to keep should have been marked Saved (kept indefinitely), or kept in email.
