# Chapter 10 solutions

## Level 1
**1.1** (1) dated copies: people and folders; (2) locking tools on one computer: history per file, one editor at a time; (3) central-server systems: one shared official history and whole-project tracking; (4) distributed systems: every user has the complete history.

## Level 2
**2.1**
| Stage | Solved | Left |
|---|---|---|
| Dated copies | Some history | Confusion, no reasons, collisions |
| Locking tools | Collisions (one editor at a time) | People wait; single computer; per-file |
| Central server | One shared history; whole projects; parallel work with merging | Needs the network; single point of failure; awkward branching |
| Distributed | Offline work; every copy a backup; cheap experiments | Larger copies; more concepts; agreement needed on the official copy |

## Level 3
A good paragraph says: the complete local history lets you read and record work without a network, makes every copy a backup, and lets you experiment privately; it also changes how teams share (by exchanging history), which shapes everything in Git.

## Level 4
**4.1** A good reply grants that popularity brings tools, teaching and hiring advantages, then asks: how many people, from where, with what network; do you need private experiments and parallel work; what large binary files exist; what must be controlled centrally; which platform will host it; and what will training cost.

## Level 5
**5.1** Examples: What is the date and what evidence supports "won"? Which other factors (platforms, network effects, free licensing) are being ignored? Who is the author and what source is cited? Is the claim about the tool or about a platform?
