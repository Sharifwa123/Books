# Chapter 65 solutions

## Level 1
**1.1** Copyright is the legal right of a creator over a work; a licence is the written permission that says what others may do with it, and under which conditions.

**1.2** GitHub's documentation says the default copyright laws apply, so the owner retains all rights and no one may reproduce, distribute or create derivative works from the work.

## Level 2
**2.1** GPL-3.0-only: strong copyleft, including larger works that use it. MIT and Apache-2.0 are permissive and ask for notices (Apache-2.0 also asks to document changes).

**2.2** Answers depend on the project. A good answer quotes the licence file for each "stated" and says "not stated" where the licence is silent.

## Level 3
**3.1** Attribution (credit the creator), NonCommercial (no use primarily intended for commercial advantage or monetary compensation), ShareAlike (adaptations under the same or a compatible licence), version 4.0.

**3.2** SPDX marks `GPL-3.0` as deprecated; the current identifiers are `GPL-3.0-only` and `GPL-3.0-or-later`.

## Level 4
**4.1** For example: "Without a licence, the default copyright law applies, which means we have no permission to copy it. I suggest we ask the owner for a licence or written permission, or write our own version, and ask the company's legal contact if unsure. Being public is not permission."

## Level 5
**5.1** Restore the notice, and check the rest of the licence's conditions. If you already distributed the code, ask the responsible person in your organization.

**5.2** Read both licence files and the compatibility statements of their projects, then ask a qualified person. Compatibility is a legal question.
