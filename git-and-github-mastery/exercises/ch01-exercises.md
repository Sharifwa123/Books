# Chapter 1 exercises — What a Computer Is

Attempt each exercise before you open `solutions/ch01-solutions.md`. None of these exercises can damage your computer.

## Level 1 — Guided

**1.1 Find your operating system.**
1. Open your computer's settings.
2. Look for a page named something like "About", "System" or "System information". (The name and place differ between systems; if you cannot find it, search the settings for the word "About".)
3. Write down: the name of the operating system, and its version.

*Expected result:* one line such as "Windows, version …", "macOS, version …" or "Linux, distribution …, version …".

## Level 2 — Partially guided

**2.1 Memory or storage?** For each situation, say whether the information is in *memory* or *storage*, and give a one-sentence reason.
- (a) A photo you took yesterday and have not deleted.
- (b) A sentence you have typed in a document but not saved.
- (c) A game you are playing right now.

**2.2 The layers.** Draw the three-layer picture (you, applications, operating system, hardware) from section 1.3.1 from memory. Then add one real example to each layer from your own device.

## Level 3 — Independent

**3.1** Explain to a friend who has never used a computer why "Save" matters. Use the kitchen picture, but do not use the words "memory" or "storage" in your explanation.

## Level 4 — Professional scenario

**4.1** A colleague asks you to install a free "PDF converter" that they found through an advertisement. It asks for administrator permission. Write down the steps you would take *before* installing anything, and the two questions you would ask your colleague.

## Level 5 — Troubleshooting

**5.1** A learner says: "I typed my essay for two hours. The power cut out. I switched the computer back on and the essay is gone, but all my saved photos are fine. My computer must be broken."
- (a) Is the computer broken? Explain what probably happened, using the words *memory* and *storage*.
- (b) Give two habits that would prevent the loss next time.
