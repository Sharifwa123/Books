# Chapter 5 exercises — The Internet, the Web and Servers

Attempt each exercise before you open `solutions/ch05-solutions.md`.

## Level 1 — Guided

**1.1 Read an address.** In your browser, open any public web page. Copy its address from the address bar into a document and label the scheme, host and path. *Expected result:* at least three labelled parts.

**1.2 Watch the status.** Open a page whose address you deliberately mistype (for example add `xyz` to the end of a real page's path). *Expected result:* a "not found" or "404"-style page. Say which side (client or server) produced that message.

## Level 2 — Partially guided

**2.1 Client or server?** For each item say whether it is acting as client or server: (a) your browser opening a page; (b) the computer that sends the page; (c) a phone app asking for the weather; (d) a friend's laptop running the Python demo of section 5.8 while you connect to it.

**2.2 Local or remote?** Sort into local and remote: a photo on your phone; a document in an online office suite; a page on `example.org`; `http://localhost:8765/`.

## Level 3 — Independent

**3.1** Explain to a friend the difference between the internet and the web, using a picture of your own (not roads) as the analogy.

## Level 4 — Professional scenario

**4.1** A colleague says: "The site has a padlock, so we can trust what it asks for." Write a reply of 4–6 sentences that explains what the padlock does and does not tell you, and lists two things to check instead.

## Level 5 — Troubleshooting

**5.1** A learner types `https://sunrise-bakery.example.org/menu.html` and sees an error saying the server cannot be found, before any page appears. Which of the ideas in this chapter (domain names, DNS, server, client) would you check first, and in what order?

**5.2** A page loads but its pictures are missing, and the browser reports `404` for `images/logo.svg`. What does the code tell you, and what would you check in the project folder? (Chapter 2<!--ref:files--> will help.)
