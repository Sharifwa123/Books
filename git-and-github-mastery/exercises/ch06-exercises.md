# Chapter 6 exercises — Accounts, Passwords and Trust

Attempt each exercise before you open `solutions/ch06-solutions.md`. Never type a real password, code or key into any exercise document.

## Level 1 — Guided

**1.1 Audit one account.** Choose one important account of your own (for example email). Without writing down any secret, record: (a) whether two-factor authentication is on; (b) what second factor is used; (c) whether you have saved recovery codes. *Expected result:* three short answers.

**1.2 Read a password rule.** A service says "Passwords must be at least 8 characters with a capital letter and a number". Explain, in two sentences, why a 20-character passphrase of four unrelated words might still be a stronger choice.

## Level 2 — Partially guided

**2.1 Authentication or authorization?** For each, say which idea it is and give the cure. (a) "Incorrect password." (b) "You do not have permission to delete this file." (c) "Enter the code sent to your phone." (d) "This repository is private."

**2.2 The hotel.** Draw your own analogy for authentication and authorization using a school, a library or a gym.

## Level 3 — Independent

**3.1** Explain to a friend how a key pair proves your identity without sending a secret. Use the padlock picture, but do not use the words "private" or "public".

## Level 4 — Professional scenario

**4.1** A three-person team will share one tool account that has access to all of the company's projects. Two of them are contractors. Write a short plan that applies least privilege and does not share passwords. Say what each person should have and what you would do when a contractor leaves.

## Level 5 — Troubleshooting

**5.1** A colleague says: "I can log in, but the page says I am not allowed to change the settings." Decide whether this is an authentication or authorization problem, and say who can fix it.

**5.2** A learner lost their phone and cannot get the 2FA codes for their account. They did not save recovery codes. List the steps you would suggest, in order, and one thing to do differently next time.
